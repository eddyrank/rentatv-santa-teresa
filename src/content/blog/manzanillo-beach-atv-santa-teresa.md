North of Playa Hermosa the road gets quiet fast. Fewer villas, fewer motorbikes, more jungle pressing in on both sides. That stretch leads up to Manzanillo, a small beach most visitors never see because it does not show up on the same lists as [Montezuma](/blog/montezuma-waterfall-atv-santa-teresa) or [Cabo Blanco](/blog/cabo-blanco-reserve-atv). I like sending people there on a slow afternoon after they have already hit the obvious stops and want somewhere with nobody else on it.

## The ride up to Manzanillo

Manzanillo is about 14 km north of Santa Teresa, which works out to roughly 35 minutes on an ATV depending on conditions. The road past Playa Hermosa is unpaved the rest of the way, and it is one of the routes I talk through with riders before they leave town. In the dry season, December to April, it is dusty and corrugated. In the green season, May to November, expect ruts and the odd muddy stretch after rain.

A 4x4 quad handles this without much drama if you ride it at a sensible pace. Slow down over the washboard sections and anything that looks like standing water, and you will be fine. This is not a road I would take at speed, and it is not one I would try in a two-wheel-drive rental car.

## What's actually out there

Manzanillo itself is a small beach backed by forest, with a headland that juts into the water at one end. There is no row of restaurants or beach bars waiting for you. Bring your own water and something to eat if you want to stay a while, because you cannot count on finding much once you arrive.

The appeal is the quiet. On most days you get long stretches of sand with hardly anyone on it, which is a different experience from [the busier beaches closer to town](/blog/exploring-santa-teresa-beaches-by-atv-safely). The same rule applies here as everywhere along this coast: park the quad off the sand and walk in. Riding on the beach itself is not allowed, and I do not make exceptions for it.

## Timing it right

I tell people to leave Santa Teresa by early-to-mid afternoon at the latest if Manzanillo is the plan for the day, so there is time to enjoy the beach and still ride back while it is light. The road north has no streetlights, and riding it after dark is not something I recommend for a first-timer.

I deliver the quad to your hotel in Santa Teresa, Playa Carmen or Mal País with a full tank, so the Manzanillo run can start right from your door. The locking cargo box on the back is enough for towels, water and a dry shirt, which is all you really need for a beach you are mostly visiting to sit on. Rates start at $65 a day, dropping to $60 a day if you are renting for three to seven days.

## Conclusion

Manzanillo is not a must-see the way Montezuma or Cabo Blanco are, but that is exactly why I like recommending it. It is a quiet beach at the end of an honest dirt road, best saved for a day when you already have a quad and want somewhere with fewer people. Go with daylight to spare, bring what you need, and you will have one of the emptier stretches of this coast to yourself.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is Playa Manzanillo worth the ride from Santa Teresa?

If you want a quiet beach without the crowds, yes. It does not have the waterfall or the protected reserve that draw people to Montezuma and Cabo Blanco, so it suits visitors who already know the area and want somewhere emptier for an afternoon.

### How far is Manzanillo from Santa Teresa by ATV?

About 14 km, which takes roughly 35 minutes depending on road conditions. The route runs north past Playa Hermosa.

### Is the road to Manzanillo paved?

No. Past Playa Hermosa the road is unpaved the rest of the way to Manzanillo. It is dusty and corrugated in the dry season and can get rutted and muddy in the green season.

### Can you swim at Manzanillo?

There is no lifeguard and no amenities, so check the conditions yourself before going in and do not swim alone. If the water looks rough, it is easy enough to enjoy the beach from the sand instead.
