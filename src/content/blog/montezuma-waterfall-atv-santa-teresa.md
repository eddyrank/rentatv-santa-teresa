The first time I rode to Montezuma waterfall by ATV, I made the classic mistake. I left Santa Teresa at noon, stopped for a long lunch in town, and reached the pool just as the light was going orange. Beautiful. Then I spent the ride home racing the dark. Now I tell everyone the same thing: this is a morning trip, and it is one of the best days out you can have from here on a quad.

Montezuma sits on the other side of the peninsula, about 22 km from Santa Teresa. The falls are a short hike from the edge of town: three cascades stacked up a jungle river, with a big swimming pool under the main drop. You do not need a guide or a tour. You need a quad, closed shoes, and a sensible start time.

## The route from Santa Teresa to Montezuma

I take the main road every time. From Santa Teresa it is paved all the way inland to Cóbano, which is also where you will find the gas station, so it is the natural place to top up. From Cóbano the road drops toward the coast and Montezuma. That last stretch is steep and winding, the surface changes, and in the green season it gets muddy and rutted. Take the descent slowly and stay in low speed on the bends.

Door to door it is roughly 50 minutes at a relaxed pace. On the Can-Am Outlander 450 that is a comfortable ride, and because it is fully automatic there are no gears to think about on the hills.

You will see shorter lines on the map through Mal País and Cabuya. I do not send renters that way. Those back roads have steep climbs, loose rock and river crossings that can rise fast after rain. They look like a shortcut and ride like a test. If you want more on how the roads around here connect, my guide to [getting around Santa Teresa](/blog/getting-around-santa-teresa) covers the whole network.

## Parking and the hike to the falls

Ride through Montezuma village and follow the road south toward Cabuya. Just past the bridge on the edge of town there is a parking lot at the trailhead. Someone usually collects a small fee in cash (around ₡2,000 last time I went). Park the quad, lock your things in the cargo box, and take the helmets with you or lock them to the rack.

From there it is a 20 to 30 minute walk upriver to the main fall. The first part is a proper trail. The second part is the river itself: you hop across boulders and wade through shallow sections, so the rocks are wet and slippery. This is where flip-flops end badly. I wear old trainers or water shoes and accept that they will get soaked.

The main fall is the tallest of the three, dropping into a wide, deep pool. The two upper falls are reached by a steep set of stairs and trail from the main pool, and there can be a small extra fee at the top. Travel guides report anything from free to a few thousand colones depending on the route and who is on duty, so [carry some cash for the upper trail](https://www.twoweeksincostarica.com/montezuma-waterfalls/) and treat the exact amount as a moving target.

## Staying safe at the waterfall

I will be blunt about this part. You will see people jumping from the rocks and swinging off ropes above the pools. People have been badly hurt and killed doing exactly that here. Depth changes, rocks hide under the surface, and the ledges are slick. Swim in the pool, enjoy the spray, and leave the jumping to someone else.

The river is also a different animal in the green season. After heavy rain the water rises and turns brown, the current picks up, and the river crossing on the hike gets dangerous. If it rained hard overnight or the water looks murky and fast, skip the swim and save the falls for a dry morning.

## Make a day of it in Montezuma

The falls take two to three hours with the hike and a swim. That leaves time for the rest of Montezuma, which has a very different feel from Santa Teresa: smaller, slower, with a main beach right in front of the village and a string of quieter coves along the coast.

My usual plan: leave Santa Teresa around 8, be at the trailhead before the crowds arrive mid-morning, hike and swim, then ride back into the village for lunch and an hour on the beach. Fill up in Cóbano on the way home and you are back in Santa Teresa by mid-afternoon with daylight to spare. The ride back is always slower than the ride out. Your legs are tired and the sun is in a different place.

## Conclusion

Montezuma waterfall is the day trip I recommend most to people staying in Santa Teresa. The ride is long enough to feel like an adventure and short enough to fit around a morning surf. Take the main road through Cóbano, go early, wear real shoes, and treat the river with respect, and it is an easy, memorable day.

A quad makes it simple because you leave when you want and stop where you like. ## Frequently asked questions

### How long does it take to ride from Santa Teresa to Montezuma waterfall by ATV?

About 50 minutes for the 22 km to Montezuma via Cóbano, plus a 20 to 30 minute hike from the trailhead to the main fall. Budget half a day for the waterfall alone, or a full day if you add lunch and the beach.

### Is the road to Montezuma suitable for first-time ATV riders?

The main road through Cóbano is the right choice for beginners. The descent into Montezuma is steep, so ride it slowly. Avoid the back roads through Mal País and Cabuya, which have steep climbs and river crossings.

### Do I need a guide to visit Montezuma waterfall?

No. The trail from the parking lot is easy to follow, and you can visit the falls on your own. Go early, stay on the trail, and ask locally about conditions if it has been raining.

### Can I swim at Montezuma waterfall?

Yes, the pool under the main fall is popular for swimming when the river is calm. Stay out after heavy rain, when the current gets strong, and do not jump from the rocks or rope swings.
