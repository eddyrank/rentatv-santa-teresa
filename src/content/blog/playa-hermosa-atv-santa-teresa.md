The first time I rode north to Playa Hermosa, I was back in town before my coffee had gone cold. It is that close. Playa Hermosa sits about 5 km north of Santa Teresa, and it is where I send people on their first afternoon with a quad: long beach, a lot of palms, far fewer people than the main stretch in town, and a ride short enough to get comfortable on the machine before trying anything longer.

## Make sure it is the right Playa Hermosa

Costa Rica has several beaches called Playa Hermosa. The best known are in Guanacaste and near Jacó, both hours away from here. Every year someone types "Playa Hermosa" into a map app and gets a route that would take most of a day.

The one I mean is on the Nicoya Peninsula, just north of Santa Teresa and Playa Carmen. If a surf lesson, booking or map pin says only "Playa Hermosa", check that Santa Teresa or Cóbano shows up somewhere nearby before you trust it.

## The ride north from Santa Teresa

From the middle of Santa Teresa it is about 12 minutes by ATV. The main road through town is paved, but heading north toward Playa Hermosa you hit unpaved sections, and past Hermosa it is dirt the rest of the way up to [Manzanillo](/blog/manzanillo-beach-atv-santa-teresa). Add the side tracks down to the beach and you have a short ride that still asks you to pay attention.

Those unpaved stretches change with the season. In the dry months, December to April, they are dusty and corrugated. In the green season, May to November, low spots hold water and the edges go soft after rain. Slow down before the loose gravel, not on it. Watch for walkers, bikes and dogs near the hotel entrances, because someone always steps out where you do not expect it.

When you get there, park the quad off the road on a firm patch, without blocking a driveway or a footpath, and walk the last bit. Riding on the sand is not allowed anywhere on this coast, even where you can see someone else's tracks. I cover that rule and the access points in more detail in [my guide to exploring Santa Teresa beaches by ATV safely](/blog/exploring-santa-teresa-beaches-by-atv-safely).

## Surf, swimming and tides

Playa Hermosa is a surf beach first. It faces open Pacific swell across a wide stretch of sand, so there is usually room to spread out, but the peaks move around as the sandbars shift. Mornings tend to be cleaner before the wind picks up. If you are learning, book a lesson with a local instructor who knows which section is working that day. Do not go by what the beach was like for a friend last week.

Swimming is a different question. There is no lifeguard and no sheltered bay. On a small day it can be lovely. On a bigger day you get heavy shorebreak and currents pulling out through the gaps between waves, and that gap is often what looks like the calmest place to get in. Watch a few sets from the sand first, keep kids close, and do not swim alone. If you get caught in a current, float, signal for help and swim parallel to the beach rather than straight in against it.

The tide changes the whole beach. Low tide opens up firm sand and moves where the waves break. High tide narrows it and pushes the shorebreak up close. Check a tide chart before you go, and if you plan to walk along the rocks, make sure you can still get back when the water comes in.

## When to go, and where to go next

The dry season brings dusty roads, strong sun and more people. The green season brings greener hills, emptier beaches and rain that usually comes later in the day. November is often somewhere in between. For a beach visit, the swell, wind and rain on the day matter more than the month, and the [Instituto Meteorológico Nacional](https://www.imn.ac.cr/) forecast is worth a look before you set out.

Because Hermosa is so close, it works well as the first stop of a longer afternoon. Carry on north to Manzanillo if you want somewhere even quieter, or head back through town and down to Mal País for sunset. Leave daylight for the return either way. The dirt roads up here have no streetlights.

## Conclusion

Playa Hermosa is the easy win of the northern coast: a short ride, a wide surf beach and a lot fewer people than town. Make sure you have the right Playa Hermosa, park off the sand, and treat the ocean with respect. If it is your first day on a quad, it is the ride I would start with.

## Frequently asked questions

### How far is Playa Hermosa from Santa Teresa?

About 5 km north, around 12 minutes by ATV. You leave town on the paved main road, then hit unpaved sections heading north and a dirt track down to the beach itself.

### Is there more than one Playa Hermosa in Costa Rica?

Yes. The best-known ones are in Guanacaste and near Jacó. The one near Santa Teresa is on the Nicoya Peninsula, just north of Playa Carmen.

### Is Playa Hermosa good for beginner surfers?

It can be on smaller days, with the right tide and a local instructor who picks the spot. On bigger swells it gets heavy and is better left to experienced surfers.

### Is it safe to swim at Playa Hermosa?

Sometimes. There is no lifeguard, and the waves and currents change with the swell and tide. Watch the water from the beach first, do not swim alone, and stay out if it looks rough.
