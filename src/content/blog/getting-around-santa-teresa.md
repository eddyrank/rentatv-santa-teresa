Santa Teresa is not a compact town. It runs several kilometres along a single unpaved road, with beaches, restaurants, surf breaks, and accommodation spread out across the whole length. Getting from one end to the other — and to the areas around Mal País, Playa Hermosa, and Playa Carmen — takes more than a short walk.

If you are planning a trip and wondering how people get around, here is what actually works.

## The roads in Santa Teresa

The main road through Santa Teresa is unpaved. In the dry season, from December to April, it is heavily corrugated and dusty. In the green season, from May to November, sections of it become soft and waterlogged after heavy rain, with potholes and mud at the lower points.

This is not a complaint — it is part of what makes Santa Teresa feel the way it does. But it means that transportation options matter more here than in a typical beach town with paved streets.

## Options for getting around

### Taxis

Taxis exist in Santa Teresa and are useful for one-off trips, particularly airport transfers or late-night rides. For daily movement around town they become expensive quickly. If you are moving between the beach, your accommodation, a restaurant, and a surf spot several times a day, taxi costs add up to more than a rental over a multi-day stay.

There is also the waiting time. Taxis here are not like ordering an Uber. You call, you wait, sometimes you wait a long time.

### Walking

Parts of Santa Teresa are walkable, particularly if you are staying close to the main strip. In cooler weather or early morning this works fine. In the heat of the middle of the day, walking a kilometre or two on a dusty road to reach a beach or restaurant is not most people's preference.

For reaching Mal País to the south, Playa Hermosa to the north, or anything involving hills and uneven tracks, walking is not realistic.

### Renting a car

Some visitors rent a car. The practical problem is that the roads leading to many accommodations, and the roads between beaches, are better suited to higher clearance vehicles. A standard rental car struggles on the rougher sections, and the cost of a daily car rental often exceeds the cost of an ATV rental — with less flexibility and more parking difficulty.

### Renting an ATV

For most visitors staying more than a day or two, renting an ATV in Santa Teresa is the most practical option. The reasons are straightforward:

**They handle the road.** A Can-Am Outlander 450 4x4 with proper ground clearance handles the unpaved main road, the muddy green-season sections, and the steep driveways leading to hillside villas without difficulty.

**They go where you want.** You can ride to Playa Carmen for breakfast, check different surf breaks along the coast, stop wherever you want, and get back before dark. The route is yours.

**They are easy to park.** There is no driving around looking for a spot. ATVs park anywhere.

**They have storage.** Every quad we rent comes with a locking cargo box on the back. Beach bags, towels, groceries, and surf accessories fit without strapping anything to the outside.

**They cost less than daily taxis for multi-day stays.** At $60 per day for a rental of three to seven days, the math works in favour of renting if you are moving around more than once or twice a day.

## How far can you actually go

From the centre of Santa Teresa you can comfortably reach:

- **Playa Carmen** — 2 km south, around 5 minutes
- **Playa Hermosa** — 5 km north, around 12 minutes
- **Mal País** — reachable along the southern coast road
- **Manzanillo** — around 14 km, 35 minutes depending on conditions
- **Montezuma** — around 22 km, approximately 50 minutes

These are reasonable day-trip distances on an ATV, not marathon rides. Most visitors use the quad primarily to move around the Santa Teresa and Mal País area and occasionally make the longer run toward Montezuma.

## What you need to rent

To rent an ATV in Santa Teresa you need a valid physical driver's licence from your home country. A photo of the licence on your phone is not accepted — the physical card is required. A standard car licence is sufficient.

You also need closed shoes. Flip-flops are not safe on a quad and rental operators will not hand over the keys if you are wearing them.

## Free hotel delivery

One practical advantage of renting from a delivery-based service rather than picking up from a fixed location is that the quad comes to you. You give your hotel or Airbnb address, the ATV arrives, the controls are explained, and you start from wherever you are staying. At the end of the rental it is collected from the same spot.

For visitors staying on the hills above Santa Teresa, or anywhere that is a walk from the main road, this saves time at both ends of the trip.
