The ride to Cabo Blanco is the one I save for guests who have already done Montezuma and want something quieter. You run out of town, out of traffic, and almost out of road. Then the forest closes in at the southern tip of the Nicoya Peninsula and you are standing at the gate of the oldest protected area in Costa Rica.

Doing Cabo Blanco Reserve by ATV is a self-drive day trip, not a tour. The quad gets you from your hotel in Santa Teresa to the reserve entrance on your own schedule. Inside, you hike. Riding inside the reserve is not allowed, and I would not want it to be.

## The route from Santa Teresa to Cabo Blanco

The reserve is about 30 km from Santa Teresa, and I allow a bit over an hour each way at a relaxed pace. I take the long way every time. From Santa Teresa the main road is paved inland to Cóbano, where you should fill the tank because there is nothing past it worth counting on. From Cóbano you drop down to Montezuma, then follow the coast road south to the small village of Cabuya. The reserve entrance is a few minutes past Cabuya at the end of the road.

Once you leave the pavement after Cóbano, the surface changes from stretch to stretch: some sections are smooth, others are rutted, and in the green season some of it turns to mud. Ride it slowly, especially the steep descent into Montezuma. If you have not been out that way before, my guide to [riding to Montezuma waterfall](/blog/montezuma-waterfall-atv-santa-teresa) covers the first two thirds of this route in detail.

There is a shorter line on the map from Mal País over the hills to Cabuya. I do not send renters that way. It has steep climbs, loose rock and river crossings, and after rain it is a different road from the one you saw on Google. It is fine for experienced riders who know it. For everyone else, the extra distance through Cóbano is worth it.

## Opening hours, fees and the trails

Cabo Blanco Absolute Natural Reserve is open Wednesday to Sunday, roughly 8am to 4pm, and closed Monday and Tuesday. Last time I checked, the entrance fee for foreign visitors was around $12 plus tax, payable at the ranger station. Hours and fees change, so confirm before you ride all the way down there. Showing up on a Monday is a long way to go for a photo of a locked gate.

The reserve protects a block of Pacific lowland tropical forest that was set aside in 1963, largely thanks to a Swedish and Danish couple who settled near Cabuya. That is where the trail names come from. The Danés trail is a shorter loop near the entrance. The [Sueco trail runs about 4.5 km down to the beach](https://www.lonelyplanet.com/points-of-interest/reserva-natural-absoluta-cabo-blanco/1201690) at Playa Cabo Blanco, and it is hilly, humid and slower than the distance suggests.

If you want to reach the beach, be at the gate by around 9 and give yourself a turnaround time. The rangers will not let you start the Sueco trail late in the day, and you do not want to be hiking back up in the afternoon heat with an empty bottle.

## Why a quad works for this trip

Cabo Blanco is far enough that a taxi there and back gets expensive, and there is no easy way to get picked up from Cabuya when you are done. With a quad you leave when you want, stop in Montezuma for a swim or lunch on the way home, and do not have to hike to a schedule. If a full day feels like too much, [the ride up to Manzanillo](/blog/manzanillo-beach-atv-santa-teresa) is the shorter version of the same idea, a quiet beach without the long drive.

The Can-Am Outlander 450 is fully automatic and 4x4, which matters on the steep, broken sections after Cóbano. The locking rear cargo box holds water, a towel and a dry shirt while you are on the trail. Two people can ride on one quad, but it is a long day in the seat for a passenger, so if you are both comfortable driving, two quads are easier on the back. Do not ride on the beaches along the way. Park at the road and walk onto the sand. The same rules from my guide to [exploring the beaches by ATV safely](/blog/exploring-santa-teresa-beaches-by-atv-safely) apply all the way down the coast.

## Conclusion

Cabo Blanco Reserve by ATV is a full day: an hour of riding each way, a few hours on the trail, and a stop in Montezuma or Cabuya if you have the energy. Go on a day the reserve is open, take the main road through Cóbano, start early, and be home before dark. It is the quietest corner of the peninsula and well worth the ride.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can you ride an ATV inside Cabo Blanco Reserve?

No. Cabo Blanco is a protected reserve and the trails are for hiking only. You ride to the entrance near Cabuya, park, and walk from there.

### How long does it take to get to Cabo Blanco from Santa Teresa by ATV?

A bit over an hour each way for roughly 30 km, via Cóbano, Montezuma and Cabuya. Add three to five hours if you want to hike the Sueco trail to the beach and back.

### Is the road to Cabo Blanco suitable for beginners?

The main route through Cóbano and Montezuma is the right choice for first-time riders, as long as you take the unpaved sections slowly. Skip the hill road from Mal País to Cabuya, which is steep and has river crossings.

### What days is Cabo Blanco Reserve open?

It is generally open Wednesday to Sunday from around 8am to 4pm and closed Monday and Tuesday. Check current hours and fees before you go, as they can change.
