Spend enough time in Santa Teresa and you start noticing the small changes that add up. I'm out on these roads every day, dropping off and picking up quads, and the town looks a little different every season. Most of it is good. Here's what stands out right now.

## The food scene keeps getting better

A few years ago, options outside the main strip were limited. That's changed. There are more small kitchens, more people cooking the food they grew up with, and more variety without needing a car to reach it. Whether it's a taco stand that opens for a few hours in the evening or a proper sit-down spot near Playa Carmen, visitors are eating better than they used to, and a lot of it is walkable or a short ride from wherever you're staying.

## Surf culture is more welcoming, not less

Santa Teresa has always been a surf town, but the vibe in the water and around it has gotten more open. There are more beginner-friendly schools, more people willing to give newcomers pointers, and less of the territorial attitude that can show up in established surf spots. It still gets crowded at the popular breaks during peak season, but the overall culture leans toward sharing the wave rather than guarding it.

## More local businesses, fewer big outside operators

Walk the main road and most of what you see is still locally run — small hotels, family restaurants, independent tour operators. That matters for a town like this. Money spent locally tends to stay local, and it shows in how invested people are in keeping the place looking and feeling the way it does. It's part of why the town hasn't lost its character the way some other coastal spots have.

## The roads are slowly improving

I mentioned this in an [earlier post about getting around Santa Teresa](/blog/getting-around-santa-teresa) — the paved stretch through the main part of town has held up well, and there's been steady, if slow, work on some of the roughest side roads. It's not dramatic, and the unpaved sections still turn to mud in the green season, but the trend over the last few years has been toward improvement rather than neglect.

## A community that looks out for the coast

Beach cleanups happen more regularly than they used to, and I see more visitors joining in rather than just locals. Small things — someone picking up a stray bottle on their way back from the beach, a hotel putting out extra bins during high season — but they add up. Santa Teresa's appeal is tied directly to how clean the beaches and water stay, and more people here seem to understand that.

## Why this matters if you're visiting

None of this is flashy news. It's the kind of steady, unglamorous progress that makes a place better to live in and better to visit — a wider range of food, a friendlier surf lineup, roads that are a bit easier to manage, and a community that treats the coastline like something worth protecting. If you're planning a trip, it means the Santa Teresa you'll find is, in a lot of small ways, an easier and better version of the one from a few years back.

Getting around to see all of it is still the same as it's always been — the town is spread out, and having your own way to move between the beach, the restaurants, and the surf spots makes a real difference. That part hasn't changed.
