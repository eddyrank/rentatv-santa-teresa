Last month someone showed up ready to ride with a photo of their licence on their phone and sandals on their feet. I turned them around until they dug the actual card out of their hotel safe and borrowed a pair of sneakers from a friend down the hall. It happens more than you would think, so before you book anything, here are the ATV rental requirements in Santa Teresa that actually matter, not the boilerplate nobody reads.

## What you need before I hand over the keys

The licence has to be physical. A photo on your phone, a scan in an email, or a description of the number does not count. I need the actual card in hand, and a standard car licence from your home country is enough. You do not need a motorcycle endorsement to ride a quad here.

Closed shoes are the other rule I do not bend on. Flip-flops and sandals do nothing to protect your feet if the quad tips at low speed on a loose corner, and I will not hand over the keys to anyone wearing them. Bring real shoes, or plan to pick up a cheap pair in town before you call me.

## The roads decide more than the paperwork does

Santa Teresa's main road through to Mal País and inland to Cóbano is paved, which surprises people who picture this whole area as one long dirt track. What is unpaved is everything else that matters for a day out: the hill roads behind town, the route north toward Manzanillo, and the stretch out toward Montezuma. In the dry season those sections are dusty and corrugated. In the green season they soften, and the low points hold water and mud.

None of that is a reason to skip a quad. It is the reason you want one, especially once you see how spread out [getting around Santa Teresa](/blog/getting-around-santa-teresa) actually is. A regular rental car struggles on the same roads a quad handles without much thought. But it does mean the requirement that matters most is not written on any form: know roughly which roads you are riding before you leave, and slow down the moment the surface changes.

## Picking the right quad for the roads you will ride

I run three models, and which one you need depends more on where you are going than how experienced you are. The Kymco 300cc is fully automatic and the easiest to start on, good for the coast road and short hops between Playa Carmen and Santa Teresa. The Honda 420cc has more power and shifts with your foot, which some riders prefer once they are comfortable. The Yamaha 450cc is the one I put people on for the hill roads, [the ride up to Manzanillo](/blog/manzanillo-beach-atv-santa-teresa), and anything in the green season, because the extra power and clearance matter most exactly where the road gets worst.

## Where you can't ride, no matter what

Driving on the beach is against the law here, not a rule I made up to cover myself. You will see the signs posted at the main beach accesses. Ride the road down to the sand, park, and walk the rest of the way. I have had guests argue that a rental agreement should get them around this. It does not, and I am not going to be the one who gets fined for it. My guide to [exploring Santa Teresa beaches by ATV safely](/blog/exploring-santa-teresa-beaches-by-atv-safely) covers exactly where the road ends and the sand begins, beach by beach.

## Conclusion

The real requirements to rent an ATV in Santa Teresa are shorter than the paperwork makes them look: a physical licence, closed shoes, a rough plan for which roads you are riding, and staying off the sand. Get those right and the rest is just picking a quad and a delivery address. Message me with your dates and where you are staying, and I will walk you through the rest before I bring one to your door.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need a licence to rent an ATV in Santa Teresa?

Yes. You need a valid physical driver's licence from your home country. A standard car licence is enough, and I check for the physical card, not a photo of it, before you ride.

### Will a US or Canadian driver's licence work?

Yes. A standard car licence from the US, Canada, or most other countries is accepted. Just make sure you bring the actual card, not a picture of it on your phone.

### Can I ride an ATV on the beach in Santa Teresa?

No. Driving on the beach is against the law in Costa Rica, and it is enforced here. Ride to the beach on the road, then park and walk onto the sand.

### What should I wear to ride?

Closed shoes, always. Flip-flops and sandals are not safe on a quad and I will not hand over the keys if you are wearing them. Bring real shoes or pick up a cheap pair in town before pickup.
